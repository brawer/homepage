This one joins my [Memes](/tags/memes/) series — internet memes
reimagined in oil.

_Paranoid Parrot_ is the go-to reaction image for that special 3 a.m.
thought spiral: a coworker says “sounds good” instead of “sounds
great,” and within four seconds you’ve reconstructed an entire
conspiracy against you. Not a spiral I personally recognize, for what
it’s worth — I’ve got my own peculiar collection of quirks, just
different ones.

What I’m genuinely proud of here is the beak, built up the
traditional way: [fat over
lean](https://nersel.com/pages/the-5-stages-of-the-old-masters-oil-painting-process),
each layer a touch oilier than the last, so the paint stays flexible
instead of cracking apart. Why bother? It lets you stack [many
translucent
glazes](https://www.csmonitor.com/From-the-news-wires/2010/0716/Mona-Lisa-examination-reveals-layers-of-paint-for-dreamy-quality)
on top of each other — and in the finished painting, light doesn’t
just bounce off the surface. It travels down through that stack of
[polymerized oil](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/2193-0414-1-3.pdf),
striking pigment particles at every depth: some of it gets absorbed,
some gets reflected back out, and because this is happening across so
many layers at once, the whole surface shimmers in a way no
photograph quite manages to capture.

The catch: each layer must “dry” completely before the next goes on,
or the paint turns to mud, ruining the whole thing. I’d say it worked
out well for the beak, but the feathers could be fluffier, and the
eye isn’t quite right either — something to fix some rainy day. Or,
given how slow this technique is, a whole rainy season.
